### Keep the EventReady product strategy

The homepage must continue to communicate:

“Not a filter. A verdict.”

EventReady helps a wedding guest decide what to wear by measuring relevant colour information from their photos, interpreting that information, comparing outfit options, and giving a recommendation.

* Start My Styling
* Check an Outfit

### Major visual changes

The current EventReady homepage feels too editorial, too serif-heavy, too monochromatic, and too repetitive.

Move it toward a contemporary fashion-tech product experience.

Change the visual system substantially:

* reduce reliance on Bordeaux as the dominant colour
* establish a sophisticated neutral foundation
* introduce a distinctive accent colour used sparingly
* make typography substantially more contemporary
* use a modern sans-serif as the dominant type treatment
* use display typography only where it genuinely improves hierarchy
* avoid making every section look like a luxury magazine spread
* introduce stronger image-to-UI relationships
* introduce more asymmetry and visual tension
* vary the composition of sections instead of repeating the same structure

Do not simply recolour the current page.

### Keep the product evidence as the central story

The strongest product narrative is:

MEASURE → INTERPRET → COMPARE → VERDICT

Make that sequence visually obvious.

The measurement experience should feel like actual product technology.

Use real EventReady concepts such as:

* measured skin / hair / eye values
* colour swatches
* hex values
* palette classification
* confidence / scoring
* outfit comparison
* match / verdict

Keep “Measured from your photo” visually distinct from “Your Palette” / interpretation.

### Make the comparison proof much stronger

The “same outfit, two colours” concept should become one of the most visually important parts of the homepage.

Show the same silhouette in two colour treatments.

The visitor should immediately understand:

one colour works better,
one colour works worse,
and EventReady can explain why.

Treat this as product proof rather than a normal information card.
