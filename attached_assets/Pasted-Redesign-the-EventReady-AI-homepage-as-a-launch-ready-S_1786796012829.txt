Redesign the EventReady AI homepage as a launch-ready SaaS marketing experience whose primary goal is conversion, while also making the product feel distinctive enough for a public launch, accelerator applications, and investor evaluation.

EventReady AI is a wedding-guest outfit decision assistant. A user provides photos, the product measures their skin, hair, and eye colours, determines a colour palette, evaluates outfit colours and styles, and gives a clear recommendation with supporting evidence. The core positioning is:

“Not a filter. A verdict.”

The website should communicate that EventReady does not merely generate or visualize outfits. Its main value is helping someone decide what to wear with confidence.

The homepage currently has three important entry paths and these must remain prominent:

1. Start My Styling — for users who want EventReady to choose and style an outfit for them.
2. Check an Outfit — for users who have already chosen clothing and want to know whether it works for them.
3. Try Demo Persona — for users who want to experience the product immediately without uploading photos.

Do not redesign this as a generic AI SaaS landing page, generic fashion ecommerce page, or dashboard.

The design direction should be premium, contemporary, fashion-tech, cinematic, and highly visual. We are considering a scroll-driven pseudo-3D “world” as part of the experience. The homepage should therefore be designed around the idea that scrolling can tell the EventReady story visually rather than simply revealing disconnected sections.

However, the cinematic experience must support conversion rather than exist only as visual decoration.

The ideal narrative is:

uncertainty → measurement → interpretation → comparison → verdict → action

The opening experience should make a visitor immediately understand the problem:
“I have an event. I don't know what to wear. EventReady can actually tell me.”

Explore a cinematic, pseudo-3D hero experience that could later be powered by pre-rendered AI video and scroll-scrubbing. For now, build the complete interaction and visual structure using high-quality placeholders or generated-style visual treatments rather than requiring the final video assets.

The pseudo-3D hero should feel like one continuous visual journey rather than a collection of unrelated animations. It should be possible later to replace the placeholder visual sequence with connected scroll-scrubbed video clips.

The hero should communicate the product transformation visually:
a person / outfit → their colour information being measured → colour options being evaluated → a comparison between colours → a final recommendation.

Avoid excessive 3D effects, random floating cards, parallax for the sake of parallax, or animations that distract from the message.

After the cinematic opening, transition into clean, highly polished product sections that make the product understandable and trustworthy.

The page should include strong sections around:

* What EventReady actually measures.
* Why the recommendation is different from ordinary outfit generators.
* The “same silhouette, two colours” proof concept.
* A realistic preview of the final recommendation / result experience.
* How the process works from photo to verdict.
* The three ways to use the product.
* A strong conversion section leading back to Start My Styling.
* A concise final CTA.

Use modern asymmetric layouts, large image surfaces, layered composition, generous whitespace, subtle borders, and sophisticated typography. Bento-style layouts may be used selectively where they help explain evidence or product capabilities, but do not make the entire homepage a grid of cards.

The visual system should feel cohesive and premium. It should be possible to introduce a distinctive EventReady brand colour later, so do not hard-code the existing Bordeaux palette as an unquestionable final decision.

Use the existing EventReady copy and product language as the source of truth where appropriate. Preserve the distinction between:
“Measured from your photo”
and
“Your Palette”
because measurement and interpretation are deliberately different concepts.

Trust is a major conversion requirement. The UI should communicate that EventReady does not invent a colour answer when a photo cannot be measured reliably. Failure/degraded states should therefore feel intentional and trustworthy rather than hidden.

Design for:

* desktop first
* strong mobile adaptation
* accessibility
* fast perceived performance
* reduced-motion fallback
* a future scroll-scrubbed video implementation

Do not implement a full video-generation pipeline yet.

First create the complete homepage experience and visual system so that we can evaluate:

1. whether the pseudo-3D concept is actually helping the product,
2. whether the page feels modern and premium,
3. whether the three entry actions are obvious,
4. whether the value proposition is immediately understandable,
5. whether the experience feels credible enough for a paid SaaS product.

Do not preserve the existing visual design merely because it already exists. Improve the design substantially where necessary.

Most importantly, optimize for the combination of:
premium visual identity + product clarity + trust + conversion.

The final result should feel like a serious product launch website, not a design experiment.
